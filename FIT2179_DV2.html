The Long View

A Century of Rising Heat

Australia's average temperature has risen 1.7°C since 1910. The warming accelerated sharply since the 1980s, and the most recent decade is the hottest on record. But this heat is not spread evenly — some parts of the country are warming far faster than others.

Key Finding

Every year since 1997 has been above the 1961–1990 average — 27 consecutive years of above-average heat. 2023 is now the hottest year ever recorded at +1.53°C above baseline.

💡 Each decade since the 1980s has been warmer than the last. The 2010s averaged +0.70°C, double the 1990s figure.

Across the Continent

Where is Australia Warming Fastest?

Warming is not uniform across the continent. Inland regions heat faster than coastal areas. The Northern Territory is the hottest state, Tasmania the coolest. Geography sets the baseline — but the calendar decides when that heat arrives.

Australia's North is Nearly 20°C Hotter Than its South

The Northern Territory averages 34.5°C, nearly double Tasmania's 15.2°C. Each state is shaded by its mean annual maximum temperature. Hover any state for exact values. Data: BOM ACORN-SAT 2020–2024 averages.

💡 The Northern Territory records 34.5°C average maximum, nearly double Tasmania's 15.2°C. The north-south temperature gradient spans more than 19°C.

Inland Stations Are Warming Up to Twice as Fast as Coastal Ones

A proportional symbol map places a circle at each station location. Size and colour both encode the warming rate (°C per decade since 1960). Larger darker circles warm faster. Data: BOM ACORN-SAT network.

💡 Inland stations such as Alice Springs and Longreach warm fastest. Coastal ocean moderates warming at nearby stations.

Darwin Averages 17°C Hotter Than Hobart Every Year

The coloured bar shows each city's average maximum temperature against its historical variability range (grey band). The white tick marks the exact value. Darwin and Hobart sit 17°C apart. Data: BOM long-term station records.

💡 Darwin is nearly 17°C hotter than Hobart, the widest capital city climate range in Australia.

Through the Seasons

Which Seasons Are Changing Most?

Summers are warming nearly twice as fast as winters. The fire danger season is lengthening as springs arrive earlier and autumns stay hotter. And as the seasons warm, the extremes at the edges grow more dangerous still.

Every Month of the Year in Sydney is Now Hotter Than in the 1960s

Average maximum temperature per month across four decades. Darker orange-brown means hotter. Warming is visible in every month. BOM Station 066062.

💡 January and February warmed by roughly 2.8°C from the 1960s to the 2020s. No month has escaped the trend.

Summers Are Warming Nearly Twice as Fast as Winters

Each band is one season; hover to highlight it. The widening gap between the summer and winter bands shows summer warming outpacing every other season. Data: BOM ACORN-SAT.

💡 Summer temperatures rose roughly 1.2°C since 1960, nearly double the rate of winter warming.

Melbourne's Entire Summer Heat Distribution is Shifting Hotter

Each tick is one summer day's maximum at Melbourne Station 086071. The whole spread of temperatures shifts rightward decade by decade — the hottest days are getting much hotter, not just the average.

💡 The 2020s show many more days above 38°C than any earlier decade. The whole distribution is shifting, not just the average.

The New Extremes

More Heat, Less Rain

Extreme heat days are rising across all cities. Perth's rainfall has dropped 20% since 2010. Hotter cities consistently receive less rain.

Alice Springs Endures Nearly One Month of Extreme Heat Every Year

Mean annual days exceeding 40°C per city, based on BOM climate records 2015–2024. Alice Springs shown for inland context.

💡 Adelaide and Perth average over 10 days above 40°C yearly. Alice Springs averages 28.6 days, nearly a month of extreme heat.

Perth Has Lost 20% of its Annual Rainfall Since 2010

Annual rainfall totals for selected cities. Perth shows the clearest long-term decline. Data: Kaggle Rain in Australia, BOM origin.

💡 Perth's annual rainfall fell roughly 147mm since 2010, a 20% decline straining water supply and agriculture.

Australia's Hottest Inland Cities Are Also its Driest

Each point is a city, positioned by its average maximum temperature and annual rainfall. The dashed regression line shows the overall trend. Tropical cities such as Darwin and Cairns are hot and wet, but the hot inland cities — Alice Springs, Kalgoorlie, Port Augusta — cluster in the dry lower-right. Sources: BOM ACORN-SAT and Kaggle Rain in Australia.

💡 The hottest inland cities receive under 350mm of rain a year, while temperate coastal capitals get far more. Rising inland temperatures threaten to deepen this drought divide.
